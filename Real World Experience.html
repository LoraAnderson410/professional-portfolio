Professional Solutions

Every organization has different challenges, resources, and limitations. I enjoy figuring out what's actually causing a problem, understanding what an organization needs, and finding a solution that works within those realities.

Sometimes that means implementing a new system. Other times, it means getting creative with the tools already available. The solutions featured here aren't about using the most sophisticated technology. They're about finding practical ways to make things work better.

Featured Case Studies

Two examples of workplace challenges where I designed and implemented solutions using the resources, people, and systems available. Each includes a detailed case study.

Employee Experience & Retention

Redesigning the First 90 Days

A residential youth services program was experiencing significant early turnover. The existing approach wasn't providing enough preparation and support for new staff entering a demanding environment.

I redesigned the onboarding process using structured training, volunteer peer mentors, existing orientation materials, and leadership check ins. The solution relied on better coordination and use of available resources rather than purchasing another system.

90% → 30% Approximate first 90 day turnover at one home before and after implementation. Historical estimates, not proof that the redesign alone caused the change.

Business Process Automation & Compliance

Policy Lifecycle Management & Automation

Tracking hundreds of policies through reviews, approvals, and publication depended on manual reminders and administrative follow up. Important steps could be missed when workloads changed or staff were unavailable.

Rather than investing in a separate policy management platform, I used existing Microsoft 365 tools to build interconnected workflows. Routine administrative tasks were automated while substantive compliance review and board approval remained human responsibilities.

~300 Policies Supported by workflows operating for approximately two years. Time savings were not formally measured.

Practical Problem Solving

Problems Solved With Practical Thinking

These are additional examples of identifying operational needs and developing workable solutions. Not every problem requires new software or a larger budget. Often the challenge is figuring out how to make better use of what's already available.

Safety & Facilities

Emergency Evacuation Map Redesign

Existing evacuation maps were hand drawn, difficult to follow, and visually inconsistent. Specialized mapping software wasn't financially justified for the organization. I used Excel to create clearer, more professional evacuation maps using software we already had.

Facilities & Operations

Maintenance Request Tracking

Maintenance work needed a more consistent way to capture and track requests. A dedicated maintenance portal wasn't an available option, so I developed an alternative tracking approach using existing resources. The goal was to improve visibility into outstanding work without adding another paid system.

IT Governance & Information Management

SharePoint Access Audit & Cleanup

SharePoint files and access permissions needed a thorough review, but our available auditing and cleanup options were limited. I developed a PowerShell script to identify files and access information, then led the manual cleanup of the findings. It required combining technical analysis with detailed hands on work.

Human Resources & Technology

HRIS Implementation & Process Change

Human resources systems need to support the way an organization actually operates, not simply reproduce outdated procedures. I've worked on HRIS implementations and process changes involving UKG, ADP Workforce Now, and Paychex. My focus has been on aligning technology with operational requirements, employee information, and the people using the systems.

Vendor Management & Administration

Contract Lifecycle Management

Vendor agreements and renewal dates can become difficult to manage when information is scattered or depends on someone remembering an upcoming deadline. I developed a centralized contract tracking process with reminders using available tools. This provided a more structured way to review agreements and plan for renewal decisions.

Analytics & Decision Support

Operational Performance Dashboards

Organizations often collect plenty of information without having an effective way to use it. I've developed Excel and Power BI reporting tools to bring operational and workforce measures into a more useful format. The goal was to help leadership see patterns, ask better questions, and make more informed decisions.

Property Operations & Technology

Property Management System Implementation

Paper based processes made property information and routine administrative work harder to manage consistently. I participated in selecting and implementing Buildium as a more centralized approach. The work involved understanding operational requirements and helping transition existing practices into a digital system.

Safety & Compliance

Practical Safety & Compliance Processes

Written policies and completed forms don't automatically create a safe workplace. I've worked on training, safety processes, and compliance practices that needed to function in daily operations, not just on paper. My approach has been to consider how responsibilities are communicated, completed, and followed through.

Workforce Planning & Operations

Workforce Scheduling & Staffing Processes

Staffing challenges often involve more than filling an empty shift. Coverage requirements, employee availability, training, and operational demands all affect workable schedules. I've coordinated staffing approaches that accounted for those competing needs and supported continuity of services.

The Right Solution for the Right Problem

I don't believe every challenge needs a new platform, a consultant, or a larger budget. Sometimes those investments are necessary. Sometimes the right answer is a spreadsheet, a better process, a small automation, or simply connecting information that already exists.

What matters is understanding the actual problem, working within the organization's resources and requirements, and building something people can realistically use and maintain.

These examples are drawn from my professional experience. Confidential organizational information and proprietary materials have been excluded. The featured case studies provide more detail about their implementation, results, and limitations.
