About Me

Curious by nature. Solution focused by habit.

My background spans operations, compliance, administration, process improvement, and people leadership. Regardless of the role, I tend to do the same thing: understand how the work is actually happening, identify what is getting in the way, and build practical solutions that make it work better.

I'm also a lifelong learner. If I don't know how to do something, I'm usually more interested in figuring it out than avoiding it. This is a place to share some of what I've built along the way, whether it started with a business problem, something I wanted to learn, or simply a question I couldn't leave alone.

Featured Case Study

Turning information into operational decisions.

Northstar IT

From Data to Operational Decisions

I created Northstar IT because I wanted a realistic environment where I could keep building my data skills while working through the kinds of operational problems I actually enjoy solving.

Northstar is a fictional IT company with people management and operational challenges. I'm building the synthetic data behind the company, then using Excel, SQL, and Power BI to clean it, explore it, and turn it into useful information. The end goal isn't just a dashboard. It's to use the data to identify problems, make operational recommendations, and create the kind of reporting leadership could actually use to make decisions.

Problems I Couldn't Leave Alone

Some projects start because I'm assigned a problem. Others start because I encounter something that makes me wonder, “Why does this work this way?”

I'm naturally solution focused. I'd rather put my energy into figuring out how something could work better than continue being annoyed by the problem.

When a Process Becomes the Project

My experience navigating Oregon's unemployment process left me with a lot of questions about the process itself. After enough confusing instructions, communication gaps, and dead ends, my operations brain took over.

It was easier to start mapping the process and thinking about how it could work better than to keep getting annoyed by it. The result is an independent look at the user journey, friction points, communication gaps, and opportunities for improvement.

Thor's Allergy Tracker

Thor, my Catahoula Beagle mix, developed a nasty skin allergy that we haven't been able to pin down. With food, medication, grooming, weather, and environmental factors all potentially playing a role, “I think it might be worse” wasn't giving us much useful information.

So naturally, I decided to build a spreadsheet.

The goal is to track his symptoms alongside the things that could be influencing them, look for patterns over time, and give his vet something more useful to work with than my memory.
